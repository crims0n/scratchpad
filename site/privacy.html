Privacy Policy

Effective November 7, 2026

Scratchpad is a local-first note editor. This policy covers every edition of the app: the Mac App Store edition (listed as Scratchpad Notes) and the editions downloaded from this website or GitHub. Scratchpad does not collect, transmit, sell or share your personal data. It has no accounts, analytics, advertising, tracking or sync service.

Your notes stay on your device

Notes, folders, trash and settings are stored on your device. They are kept in the app's own storage, or in workspace, export and backup files that you choose. They are never sent to the developer or to anyone else, and you can delete them at any time.

When Scratchpad uses the network

  • Update checks exist only in the editions downloaded from this website or GitHub. They are off by default. When you check for updates, the app requests a public release file from GitHub Pages. GitHub receives normal connection information such as your IP address, but no notes, workspace data or identifiers. The Mac App Store edition has no update check, because the App Store delivers its updates.
  • Agent access is optional and off until you turn it on. It lets AI tools on your own computer read or change notes you allow, through a connection that stays on your device. Scratchpad itself sends nothing over the internet for this. A tool you connect may have its own privacy practices.
  • Links in your notes, and links to documentation, open in your web browser only when you click them. Previewing a note does not load remote images.

Information Apple may share

If you install Scratchpad from the Mac App Store or TestFlight and allow it in your Mac's settings, Apple may share crash reports and basic usage statistics with the developer. Apple handles this under its own privacy policy, and the developer uses it only to fix problems. TestFlight also shares any feedback you choose to send.

Children

Scratchpad collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new effective date. Its history is public in the project's source repository.

Contact

Ask questions by opening an issue on GitHub. Report security issues privately as described in the security policy.
